# Commercial Solutions for Floor Plan Extraction

*Research catalogue · October 2026 · Commercial products, APIs, on-premise software and services that turn existing plans (raster, PDF, DWG) into structured floor data, or that cover parts of the chain (CAD validation, area measurement, CAFM import with SAP integration), rated for BBL's use case. Open-source tools and models are in [open-source solutions](solutions-open.md); the open-source tables still at the end of this document will move there. Training data is in [open datasets](datasets-open.md) and [closed datasets](datasets-closed.md), needs and constraints in [motivation and goals](motivation-goals.md), the comparison with AxcelerateAI's published pipeline in the [pipeline and pilot review §5](reviews/2026-10-07-pipeline-and-pilot-v2.md#5-comparison-with-axcelerateai). Bracketed numbers refer to the shared [source list](../research/sources.md).*

## 1. Scope and Method

**Scope.** Commercial offers that BBL could buy or test today, in eight groups:
- plan conversion products and APIs that read raster plans, PDFs or DWGs and return rooms, walls and openings (§3.1);
- raster-to-vector software that runs on-premise (§3.2);
- construction takeoff tools that detect rooms and areas on plans (§3.3);
- indoor mapping platforms that import CAD or plan images (§3.4);
- CAD validation, area measurement and CAD toolkits (§3.5);
- CAFM/IWMS and SAP RE-FX add-ons that import plans and link rooms to SAP (§3.6);
- digitising, area measurement and scan-to-plan services, mainly in Switzerland (§3.7);
- labelling and model platforms that support BBL's own pipeline (§3.8).

The evidence on accuracy is summarised in §3.9. Research methods without released code are in the [literature review and state of the art](literature-review.md); open-source tools and models are out of scope here.

**Method.** Desk research in October 2026: vendor product, pricing, documentation, privacy and data-processing pages, plus news and independent reviews where they exist, BBL's 2025 market screening\[1\] and BBL's own vendor talks and tests. No vendor was contacted, no account was registered and no quote was requested, so unpublished prices and hosting details remain open.

**Table layout.** All tables in §3 use the same columns:
- *Task*: what the product extracts or does; *Inputs*: accepted plan formats.
- *Deployment and hosting*: SaaS, API, desktop, on-premise or service; data location as published.
- *Pricing*: as published in October 2026; "not published" means quote only.
- *Data rights*: who owns the output and whether the vendor may use uploaded plans, as stated in terms or DPAs; "not found" where the terms were read without result, "not checked" where they were not read.
- *SAP / CAFM*: published integrations with SAP RE-FX or CAFM systems.
- *Accuracy evidence*: "claim" marks vendor statements; "independent" marks third-party tests; "BBL test" marks BBL's own tests.
- *Relevance* for BBL: High = fits the hosting and SAP constraints and could be tested or procured now; Medium = useful for part of the chain, for non-sensitive plans, or once hosting is clarified; Low = wrong input or output, cloud-only outside Switzerland without an alternative, defunct, or would duplicate the SAP-based area management.

"BBL talks" marks BBL's own experience with the vendor.

## 2. Key Findings

1. **No product covers the chain end to end.** None found reads room stamps (AOID, usage, area) into SAP fields, derives the gross floor area per floor and runs in Switzerland. The market splits into cloud converters, takeoff tools (areas only), indoor mapping platforms (wayfinding models), on-premise vectorisers (lines only), SAP/CAFM front-ends (need clean CAD) and manual services (§3.1–§3.7).
2. **No AI converter offers Swiss hosting.** EU hosting exists (Archilogic on AWS EU West, Mappedin on Azure EU, AmpliFY in France), Swiss hosting does not. Four on-premise routes with room recognition were found, each with a caveat: AxcelerateAI builds custom pipelines on the client's servers (accuracy unverified); RasterScan ships a Docker image (vendor of unclear maturity); Archilyse's AGPL pipeline survives in forks, with commercial on-premise licences offered by its founder, but the company is in liquidation; CSoft's PlanTracer forms rooms on AutoCAD but appears to be a legacy product (§3.1, §3.2).
3. **Data rights differ more than prices.** Archilogic owns the models it generates from customer plans, CubiCasa owns its deliverables, and Bimify's terms allow training on deliverables despite "you own your data" on its website. AxcelerateAI (client owns code and weights), AmpliFY (training only on opt-in) and BIMxAI leave ownership with the customer. Any test, even on non-sensitive plans, should be preceded by a check of these terms (§3.1).
4. **All accuracy figures are vendor claims.** Pointr's own benchmark (90.2% detection, 71.5% metadata on 19 plans), Togal's "up to 98%", Kreo's 94%, AxcelerateAI's "98%" and Archilyse's "<1.2% median deviation" use undisclosed test sets. The only independent result is BBL's own AmpliFY test (no doors, all named rooms "Undefined"); AmpliFY now states that it supports residential plans only (§3.9).
5. **Room areas on PDFs are commercially mature; room stamps are not.** Takeoff tools (Togal, Kreo, STACK) detect enclosed rooms and their net and gross areas automatically, and indoor mapping platforms (Mappedin, Pointr) output rooms, doors and levels as GeoJSON or IMDF. None documents reading AOIDs or usage from room stamps, BBL's core CAFM requirement (§3.3, §3.4).
6. **The SAP end of the chain has a product category.** SAP RE-FX has a graphical-integration interface for CAD partners, and Korasoft links closed room polylines in AutoCAD, Revit or IFC to RE-FX objects inside SAP, with the drawing supplying the areas. This is the natural landing step for extraction output after plan-check, without a second area-management system. DACH CAFM suites (Planon, speedikon, pit, Kessler, eTASK) handle CAD and DIN 277 areas well, but each would be a second system, and none mentions SIA 416 (§3.6).
7. **SAP is moving real estate towards Planon.** "SAP Real Estate and Facilities Management by Planon" is an SAP solution extension, and future SAP innovation may go only there (unverified); RE-FX stays supported until at least 2040 (§3.6). Any plan front-end should be checked for a migration path.
8. **A Swiss public precedent exists for the manual route.** In 2018 Kanton Zürich awarded ICFM a "data trustee" contract after an open tender: about 1,350 floors from mixed sources and formats, CAD plans edited and migrated into SAP RE-FX, plus building-services data and five years of maintenance, for CHF 1.76 million excl. VAT (four bids up to CHF 2.92 million). It is the closest template for BBL's worst scans (§3.7).
9. **On-premise vectorisers solve a smaller problem.** VPstudio (softelec, Munich) and WiseImage (CSoft) run locally and turn scans into clean DWG lines and text, but not rooms; room formation would remain BBL's pipeline (§3.2).
10. **Microsoft's internal "AI Maps" pipeline confirms BBL's vector route.** It parses CAD with open-source libraries, normalises inconsistent names with an LLM and writes GeoJSON for more than 500 buildings; it is not sold (§3.4).

### 3.6 CAFM/IWMS and SAP RE-FX Integration

BBL wants plan data to land in SAP RE-FX, not in a second area-management system. Two kinds of products matter:
- **Graphical front-ends inside SAP** that link CAD room polygons to RE-FX objects. RE-FX has a graphical-integration package with a CAD-provider interface (`IF_RECA_CAD_PROVIDER`) for such partners (seen only in a search snippet).\[322\] In this model the drawing supplies the measurements, and the room ID in the drawing (the AOID) links each polygon to its RE-FX architectural object.\[323\]
- **CAFM suites with their own CAD module and an SAP interface.** They import DWG, compute areas to DIN 277 and push data to SAP, but each is an area-management system in its own right.

SAP's strategy matters for both. SAP sells "SAP Real Estate and Facilities Management by Planon" as a solution extension, with real-estate and FM processes, space management included, in Planon and finance in S/4HANA Contract and Lease Management.\[324\] A snippet of SAP's product page says future innovation will go only into that combination (page not reachable, unverified)\[325\]; RE-FX is expected to stay supported until at least 2040\[326\]. A plan front-end bought now should therefore write to RE-FX through documented interfaces and be checked for a migration path.

### 3.9 Accuracy Evidence

No vendor publishes independently verified accuracy figures. What exists:
- **Vendor benchmarks and claims.** Pointr's own benchmark on 19 plans: detection 90.2%, classification 81.5%, metadata 71.5%\[297\]; Togal "up to 98% on floor plans"\[269\]; Kreo 94% outline accuracy\[273\]; AxcelerateAI "98%+"; Archilyse a median deviation below 1.2%\[251\]. None states its test set, so they are not comparable.
- **Case studies.** Addnode (Bimify's part-owner): a UK architecture firm digitising PDF-only buildings reported 40% time and cost reduction vs. manual modelling (July 2025).\[92\] simulationHub reports 16 instead of 45 hours for a six-storey office from PDF to energy model with its Buildings AI.\[260\] Neither is independent and neither gives an accuracy figure.
- **BBL's own test** of AmpliFY (§3.1) is the only independent result: no doors, named rooms left "Undefined".
- **A public precedent for manual services:** Kanton Zürich's data-trustee contract (§3.7) shows the price level of the manual route, though not its accuracy.

## 4. Recommended Next Steps

1. **Test on non-sensitive plans only**, such as the public historical plans in the pilot's benchmark set, with the same harness and correction-minutes metric as the in-house pipeline ([pipeline §8](pipeline.md#8-evaluation)). Check each vendor's terms first (§2, finding 3).
   - Mappedin: the only self-service converter for raster, PDF and DWG; compare its rooms and doors with the pilot.
   - Kreo or Togal: automatic room areas on PDFs, as a reference for area error.
   - AxcelerateAI: its free one-plan analysis, then a fixed-scope proof of concept only if the result is strong.
   - RasterScan: run the Docker image locally; no data leaves BBL.
   - WiseBIM and Bimify (once Instant or the API is live): IFC route for comparison.
2. **Ask in writing about Swiss hosting or on-premise** (a request for information under procurement law, not a quote):
   - Archilogic: whether the Enterprise "private instance" can run in Switzerland, and who owns the generated models.
   - Mappedin and Pointr: Swiss or on-premise deployment, and export of rooms with external IDs (AOIDs).
   - AxcelerateAI: on-premise build in Switzerland, IP transfer, and training-data rights.
   - Archilyse's founder: terms and support for a commercial on-premise licence of the pipeline.
   - NavVis: self-hosted IVION or Swiss data residency for scanning projects.
3. **Clarify the SAP landing step with BBL's SAP team:** which RE-FX graphical integration is in place today, whether Korasoft (or another partner using the RE-FX CAD interface) can run on BBL's on-premise SAP and handle SIA 416 area types, and how the RE-FX versus Planon roadmap affects it.
4. **Prepare a service tender for the worst scans**, modelled on Kanton Zürich's data-trustee contract (§3.7). Candidates: ICFM, Walder + Trüeb, DANZ FM, ambrosia FM, Archify and Keller + Steiner. Require:
   - processing and drafting in Switzerland;
   - delivery to the CAD-Richtlinie BBL V1.0, checked by plan-check before acceptance;
   - the original scan and the compliant DWG delivered as pairs, with rights to use them as training and evaluation data.
5. **Use services as a label source.** The paired output from step 4 is labelled, in-domain data for the hardest styles. A self-hosted CVAT or Label Studio covers corrections and extra labels without sending plans out.
6. **Where no usable plan exists, buy on-site capture**: laser scanning with NavVis or similar (RAPP, NRP, Terradata, Emch+Berger, Keller + Steiner), with local processing and plans delivered to the CAD-Richtlinie.

## 5. Limitations of This Catalogue

- Desk research only. No vendor was contacted, no product was tested except AmpliFY (BBL test), and no quote was requested, so unpublished prices, hosting details and contract terms remain open.
- Accuracy figures are vendor claims unless marked otherwise; they are not comparable across rows.
- The web-search budget ran out during the research. Not covered: Alpha Takeoff, Cubit, German-language AI takeoff tools, French start-ups beyond WiseBIM, LandingAI and Azure/Google document AI, ClearEdge3D, FARO, Autodesk ReCap, a list of Swiss NavVis partners, and DACH CAFM tools such as VI-Space, dataBAT, Facilitycloud, IMS, mobilis and the SIA 416 area tools of ArchiCAD and Vectorworks.
- Several vendor pages blocked automated access or returned errors (Korasoft, SAP Store, Autodesk product pages, Esri's 2026 blog, Scan2CAD, Arkance). Facts taken only from search snippets are marked as such.
- Data-rights and AI-training clauses were found for some vendors only; "not found" does not mean the vendor makes no use of the data.
- Prices are as published in October 2026, in the currency shown; vendor surveys of CAFM prices (€ per workplace) are self-reported.
- Not legal or procurement advice. Hosting, data protection and the Information Security Act need BBL's own assessment before any plan leaves BBL.
